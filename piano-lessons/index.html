Piano Lessons in Bellingham, WA

Private piano lessons for adults, teens, and kids, taught in your home across Bellingham and Ferndale, or online. Complete beginners through advanced players.

Who these lessons are for

Lessons are open to complete beginners and returning players alike: adults, teens, and children, whether you’re picking up piano for the first time or coming back to it after years away. Instruction is built around each student’s goals, from classical foundations to simply playing music you love.

What a lesson looks like

Each lesson blends technique, repertoire, and musicianship rather than running on repertoire alone. We work on the physical side of playing, hand position, posture, and reading fluency, build a repertoire that fits where a student is and where they want to go, and develop the listening and theory skills that make music making feel like understanding rather than memorization.

In person in Bellingham, or online

In-person lessons happen at the student’s home, currently across Bellingham and Ferndale. Online lessons are available anywhere with a stable internet connection. You’ll need a device with a camera, a way for me to hear you play clearly, and a piano or keyboard set up where I can see your hands.

Lesson logistics

Lessons run in whatever length fits the student, commonly thirty, forty five, or sixty minutes, scheduled weekly at a consistent day and time. Cancellation notice is worked out when we set up a regular lesson slot. At home, a student needs a piano or keyboard with weighted keys once they’re past the very beginning stages; there’s more on instrument choice in the questions below.

Frequently asked questions

What age should a child start piano lessons?

There’s no single right age. Some children are ready around six or seven, once they can sit and focus for a full lesson; teens and adults can start at any age. Mention your child’s age when you reach out and we can talk through whether it’s a good time to start.

Do I need a piano, or is a keyboard okay?

A keyboard is fine to start, ideally one with weighted, touch-sensitive keys so technique carries over. As a student progresses, an acoustic piano or a good digital piano makes a real difference, but it isn’t required on day one.

How often should I practice?

Consistency matters more than length. A short daily practice session works better than one long session once a week. We’ll work out a practice routine that fits your schedule at your first lesson.

Do you teach adult beginners?

Yes. Adult beginners are welcome, whether you’re starting for the first time or picking piano back up after years away, and lessons are paced to fit.
